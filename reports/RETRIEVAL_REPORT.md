## 7. How to read these results (limitations)

- **Ground truth and the question-aware rules share their definition.** For metadata questions, once the question is parsed correctly, `rule_router` / `clinical_only` reproduce the ground-truth rule, so near-perfect scores are expected by construction. Phase 1 therefore (a) validates the pipeline, (b) quantifies how far common history-selection heuristics (latest, random, fixed window) fall from the visit a question actually refers to, and (c) checks that each signal alone is insufficient across question types. It does **not** show that question-aware retrieval improves downstream answers — that needs Phase 2/3.
- **The motivating example is often solved by a fixed heuristic.** Under treatment, CST mostly falls, so the highest-CST visit is the baseline in 78.7% of `worst_edema` instances (and the lowest-BCVA visit in 56.3% of `worst_vision`). The advantage of question-aware selection comes from covering *all* question types with one system, not from any single type.
- CST is used as a proxy for edema severity, and BCVA for visual function; neither is a disease-severity label.
- Biomarker labels exist only for the first and last visit of each eye, so no biomarker questions are included.
- Instances from the same eye are strongly correlated; only 19 test eyes (18 patients) contribute, hence the wide CIs.
- TREX visit numbers are ordinal; real time intervals are not used.
